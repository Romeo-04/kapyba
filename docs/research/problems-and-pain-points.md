# Problems and pain points for a dish-rating platform for karinderyas

Research date: 2026-10-22. Scope: owner pain, buyer pain, rating-platform risks, and food waste from unsold ulam. Finance-gap, insurance and general food-waste numbers are in `msme-global-problems-and-solutions.md`, `parametric-insurance-philippines.md` and `buyer-side-and-business-model.md`; not repeated here beyond one brief cite.

## Summary

1. Strongest verified evidence is for the rating mechanism itself: for independent restaurants, ratings move revenue (about 5-9% per star in Seattle, 2003-2009) and sell-outs (19 percentage points per half star in San Francisco) [Luca, HBS WP 12-016](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hbs/wpaper/12-016.html), [Anderson and Magruder, 2012](https://are.berkeley.edu/~mlanderson/pdf/Anderson%20and%20Magruder.pdf). Both are US, online-review data; transfer to a carinderia is unproven.
2. Public hygiene information changed seller behaviour: Los Angeles grade cards (1998) raised inspection scores and cut foodborne-illness hospitalisations [Jin and Leslie, QJE 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v118y2003i2p409-451..html).
3. Food-borne illness is large: WHO estimates over 866 million cases and 1.52 million deaths in 2021 [WHO, 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/food-safety). WHO does not single out street food on that page.
4. Fake and retaliatory reviews are a real, policed problem: Google blocked or removed 292 million policy-violating reviews in 2025 [Google, 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/new-ways-were-protecting-businesses-on-maps/); the FTC banned fake reviews (Aug 2024) and the UK banned them (Apr 2025).
5. Philippine plate waste is rising: 130 g per household per day in 2023 vs 77 g in 2018-2019; rice 64.9%, vegetables 12.9%, fish 9.3% [DOST-FNRI via SunStar, 2026](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/filipino-homes-waste-more-food). This is household data, not carinderia data.
6. I found no verified Philippine figure for waste, leftovers or review behaviour in carinderias specifically. That is the largest evidence gap and the first thing to validate by interview.
7. Philippine carinderia hygiene evidence is mostly small theses; Philippine foodborne-outbreak data cited here is 2005-2018.
8. Alternative-data credit for thin-file MSMEs is supported by World Bank and IFC material, but no source ties restaurant ratings to lending; keep as a later pitch, not the problem.
9. Ranked top three for a Filipino carinderia: (a) owners lack structured feedback and cannot see why customers leave, (b) buyers cannot judge dish quality, hygiene and value before spending a limited budget, (c) cooked-but-unsold ulam (waste, cost, safety).
10. Recommended problem statement: Candidate B below (feedback gap plus waste), subject to interview validation.

## 1. Owner-side pain

**No structured feedback; not knowing why customers leave.** A rating is mainly a demand lever for independents: Luca finds the effect exists only for independent restaurants and not chains, and that chains lost share as Yelp grew, suggesting reviews substitute for brand reputation [Luca, 2011, rev. 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hbs/wpaper/12-016.html). A carinderia is the independent, no-brand case. No source I fetched measures how often carinderia owners get feedback; unverified, test in interviews.

**Invisibility and exclusion from platforms.** Philippine delivery-app data found covers consumers, not merchants. A Grab-sponsored 2024 report (industry source) says delivery apps are the second most common way Filipinos discover new F&B outlets [Tribune, 2024](https://tribune.net.ph/2024/01/19/grab-dining-online-or-not), which I could not open at source; I found no figure on carinderias on these platforms. Status: unverified.

**Reputation cannot be proven to lenders or suppliers.** World Bank describes MSMEs as "thin file" borrowers because little credit data exists on them, and lists payment, utility and mobile records as alternative data [World Bank blog, 2019](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/psd/how-can-alternative-data-help-micro-small-and-medium-enterprises-msmes-access-credit-0). An IFC 2026 report on alternative data and AI for credit exists [IFC, 2026](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doc/2026/cracking-the-credit-code-alternative-data-and-ai-for-financial-inclusion-summary.pdf), but the PDF would not extract to text here, so I only know its title and a search-result summary. Customer ratings as a credit signal: no source found. Treat as hypothesis.

**Scale of the owner population.** PSA's 2023 List of Establishments counted 1,246,373 enterprises, 90.43% micro; DTI counts 190,899 MSMEs in accommodation and food service, second after wholesale and retail [DTI, 2023](https://dti.gov.ph/businesses/msmes/msmeresources/msme-statistics). This is from a search snippet of the DTI page; I did not open the page. Carinderias are not separately counted.

**Informality and permits.** Under the Sanitation Code (PD 856) a food establishment needs a permit from the local health office, and food handlers need health certificates; the IRR covers street-sold food too [FAOLEX IRR copy](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/phi210846.pdf). One LGU checklist lists carinderias with chest X-ray, urinalysis and fecalysis requirements [Morong, Rizal](https://morongrizal.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Requirements-for-Application-of-Sanitary-Permit.pdf). Requirements vary by LGU. Whether most carinderias hold permits: unverified.

## 2. Buyer-side pain

**Food safety burden.** WHO: more than 866 million foodborne illnesses and 1.52 million deaths in 2021, US$310 billion cost, 29% of the burden in children under 5 [WHO, 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/food-safety). The earlier 2015 estimate was 600 million cases and 420,000 deaths, reported by media only here; do not mix the two. The page names no street-food figure.

**Philippines.** A compilation cited via Azanza et al. (2018) covers 209 reported outbreaks, January 2005 to June 2018, with an unknown agent in about 88% of cases and food-service facilities and households more outbreak-prone. I saw this only in a search summary, not the paper: partial. Philippine carinderia microbiology studies exist (Quezon City cooked rice and Bacillus cereus, n=20, 2022; Malate utensils, 2017) but are theses or archive records; peer review unconfirmed [UP Diliman archive](https://digitalarchives.upd.edu.ph/item/58021/971), [UP Manila record](https://ds.mainlib.upd.edu.ph/Record/UP-1685523046126336159). Small samples, so illustrative only.

**School canteens.** DepEd Order 13, s. 2017 sets Green/Yellow/Red food categories for school canteens and modifies parts of DepEd Order 8, s. 2007 [DepEd DO 13 s. 2017](https://deped.gov.ph/?p=1412). It governs canteens inside schools; carinderias outside school gates are outside it. I confirmed the order's existence only; the exact rules I did not read.

**Price sensitivity.** No primary Philippine data fetched for student or worker meal budgets. Unverified.

**Information asymmetry.** Anderson and Magruder find effects are larger when alternate information is scarcer, a good fit for unrated carinderias [Anderson and Magruder, 2012](https://are.berkeley.edu/~mlanderson/pdf/Anderson%20and%20Magruder.pdf).

## 3. Ratings-specific problems

| Issue | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Ratings move revenue | One star = about 5-9% revenue; independents only; Seattle 2003-2009, Washington State revenue data [Luca](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hbs/wpaper/12-016.html) | Verified (abstract); the 2003-2009 range is from [Harvard Magazine coverage](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/print/33472?page=all) |
| Half star changes sell-outs | +19 percentage points sell out at prime times (49% relative); San Francisco; larger when information is scarce [Anderson and Magruder](https://are.berkeley.edu/~mlanderson/pdf/Anderson%20and%20Magruder.pdf) | Verified via search summary of the paper; PDF not opened |
| Consumers react to few reviews | Response depends on review count and reviewer status [Luca](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hbs/wpaper/12-016.html) | Verified. Low-count noise is therefore a real harm; I found no source giving a minimum safe count |
| Hygiene grade cards | LA 1998 grade cards raised inspection scores, made demand respond to hygiene, cut foodborne hospitalisations [Jin and Leslie 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v118y2003i2p409-451..html) | Verified from abstract. Magnitudes not read (paywall) |
| UK FHRS | 2025 audit: display 95% Wales, 92% Northern Ireland, 70% England [FSA audit 2025](https://science.food.gov.uk/article/162577-food-hygiene-rating-scheme-audit-of-display-and-business-survey-2025.pdf); FSA notes higher-rated premises are less likely to have outbreaks | Partial. Figures from search snippets; the PDF and the Welsh "5-rating up 21 points" claim in the FSA Chief Scientific Adviser report were not verified (404) |
| Singapore grading | Old A-D letter grades replaced; 2026 SAFE scheme per secondary reposts | Unverified. No source found on effect, and NEA/SFA page not fetched |
| Fake reviews, platform scale | Google 2025: 292 million reviews blocked or removed, 13 million fake Business Profiles removed; review-spike pause and owner alerts [Google, 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/new-ways-were-protecting-businesses-on-maps/) | Verified |
| Fake reviews, law | FTC final rule (announced 14 Aug 2024) bans fake and bought reviews, review suppression by threats, and fake social indicators [FTC, 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials). UK: banned practices since April 2025; fines up to 10% of global turnover [CMA via GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/fake-and-misleading-reviews-5-businesses-under-cma-investigation) | FTC verified; per-violation penalty not on the page. UK from search summary, page not opened |
| Manipulation by owners | Anderson and Magruder test for discontinuous manipulation and find none that confounds results | Verified via summary |
| Rating inflation | Ratings lose information over time as raters avoid harming sellers [Filippas, Horton and Golden, Marketing Science 2022](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/144173) | Partial: abstract and talk summaries only; marketplace (freelance) setting, not food |
| Low-rated business outcomes (closure, data) | No source verified on closure effects for a low-rated small eatery | Unverified |

Design implication: a dish-level, private-first design reduces the harm. Low-count dishes should show "not enough ratings", not a star average.

## 4. Food waste from cooked, unsold ulam

**International scale.** The world wasted 1.05 billion tonnes of food in 2022; food service 290 million tonnes (about 28%, our arithmetic) [UN News on UNEP, 2024](https://news.un.org/en/node/1148036), already cited in `msme-global-problems-and-solutions.md`.

**Philippine scale.** Verified: DOST-FNRI 2023 National Nutrition Survey, plate waste 130 g per household per day vs 77 g in 2018-2019 (+69%); rice 64.9%, vegetables 12.9%, fish 9.3%; reported 4 May 2026 [SunStar, 2026](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/filipino-homes-waste-more-food). An earlier 2018 survey paper found 66.8 g per household per day, mostly cereals (48.0 g), then fish, meat and poultry (8.9 g), vegetables (7.2 g) [BMC Public Health, 2022](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12889-022-14894-z) (seen via search snippet; page redirected). The 2018 baselines differ (66.8 vs 77 g); cause unexplained. These are household plate leftovers, not unsold cooked food at a stall.

Other figures, all partial: UNEP household estimate about 9.4 million tons for Philippine households (cited in a Senate bill), Metro Manila about 2,000 tons of food waste a day (PIDS, 2017 data) [PIDS](https://pids.gov.ph/details/news/in-the-news/food-wasted-by-the-tons-while-millions-of-filipinos-go-hungry). I did not open either source. An APEC economy report lists 40.33 kg per capita for out-of-home consumption in 2022; I could not open it and its source is unclear: unverified.

**Carinderia-specific waste: not found.** No DOST-FNRI, PSA, FAO, WWF-Philippines or DENR figure on cooked-food waste at eateries was found. The owner cost of unsold ulam is therefore unquantified; compute it from interviews (ingredient cost per dish times leftover portions).

**Which dishes waste most.** For households: rice, then vegetables and fish (above). For carinderias: unverified. Plausible hypothesis, fish and vegetables spoil first (per `msme-global-problems-and-solutions.md` and the PRD), but not sourced here.

**Cooked-food safety and reheating.** WHO Five Keys to Safer Food include "Cook thoroughly" and "Keep food at safe temperatures" [WHO, Five Keys](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241594639). The Quezon City study above looked at cooked-rice handling and Bacillus cereus, a known risk in held rice. Leftover ulam sold the next day is a safety risk as well as a margin question. Not quantified for the Philippines.

**Leftover versus sold-out asymmetry.** Leftovers show cost, while sold-out dishes hide lost demand. The PRD already notes "leftovers reveal true demand when sold out" (R4.4). Anderson and Magruder show rating changes can drive sell-outs, which means rating data and sell-out data are linked.

**Link to platform.** Dish ratings plus sold-out and leftover logs let an owner see: high rating and sold out early (cook more), low rating and leftovers (cook less or fix), high rating and leftovers (an access or price problem, not a quality one). This is a product hypothesis, not a finding.

## 5. Ranked pain points

Relevance is for a Filipino micro-eatery near schools or workplaces.

| # | Pain point | Who | Evidence | Strength | Relevance | What the platform needs to do |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No structured feedback, cannot see why customers leave | Owner | Independents depend on ratings; chains less so [Luca](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hbs/wpaper/12-016.html); carinderia-specific data none | Partial (mechanism verified, PH unverified) | High | Dish-level, 1-tap ratings with private tips to owner |
| 2 | Buyers cannot judge dish quality or value before spending | Buyer | Effects larger when information is scarce [Anderson and Magruder](https://are.berkeley.edu/~mlanderson/pdf/Anderson%20and%20Magruder.pdf) | Partial | High | Public dish ratings with minimum counts; show price next to rating |
| 3 | Cooked-but-unsold ulam: waste, cost, reheating risk | Owner, buyer | PH household plate waste 130 g/day (2023) [SunStar](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/filipino-homes-waste-more-food); UNEP global [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/node/1148036); no PH eatery data | Partial; carinderia scale unverified | High | Pair ratings with sold-out/leftover logs; suggest cooking less of weak dishes |
| 4 | Food safety and hygiene opacity | Buyer | WHO 2021 burden [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/food-safety); LA grade cards [Jin and Leslie](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v118y2003i2p409-451..html) | Verified globally; PH carinderia partial | High | Do not claim hygiene certification; show permit status only if self-declared and labelled so |
| 5 | Invisibility, no digital footprint, platform exclusion | Owner | Google 292M reviews removed shows scale of listings and abuse [Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/new-ways-were-protecting-businesses-on-maps/); no PH merchant data | Unverified for PH | Medium-High | Free listing with no commission, phone-first onboarding |
| 6 | Fake, retaliatory or low-count ratings harm owners | Owner | Google, FTC, CMA as above | Verified | High (risk to us) | Verified-visit rating, rate limits, owner reply, "not enough ratings" state |
| 7 | Reputation cannot be proven to lenders and suppliers | Owner | Thin-file borrowers [World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/psd/how-can-alternative-data-help-micro-small-and-medium-enterprises-msmes-access-credit-0) | Partial; ratings-as-credit unverified | Medium (later phase) | Build data trail; defer lending claims |
| 8 | Price versus value pressure; buyer budgets | Both | No primary PH source fetched | Unverified | High | Show price per dish; validate by interview |
| 9 | Rating inflation | Both | [Filippas et al. 2022](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/144173) | Partial | Medium | Use relative or dish-comparison prompts instead of pure stars |
| 10 | Low-rated business outcomes | Owner | None verified | Unverified | Medium | Private-first ratings; public only after enough ratings |

Placement of food waste: rank 3, because it is the only pain with direct peso impact for the owner and fits the existing forecasting product, but its carinderia-level evidence is weakest of the top three.

## 6. Candidate problem statements

Each under 40 words.

**A.** Filipino carinderias feed students and workers daily, yet owners get no structured feedback and buyers cannot judge which dish is worth their limited budget. Independent eateries depend on ratings that this sector lacks.
(Count: 35.)

**B.** Carinderia owners cook blind: no dish-level feedback on what customers like, so unsold ulam is wasted, while students and workers cannot tell which stall's dishes are worth their budget.
(Count: 31.)

**C.** Small eateries have no verifiable record of quality: buyers face food-safety and value uncertainty, and owners cannot prove reputation to suppliers or lenders. Ratings evidence from independent restaurants suggests this record matters.
(Count: 33.)

**Recommendation: B.** It names both sides, ties to the demand-forecasting work already in the PRD, and rests on a peso-denominated owner pain (waste) rather than on a lending claim with no source. Caveat: "unsold ulam is wasted" lacks carinderia-level evidence, so run the owner interviews below before using it in the pitch. C relies on the least-evidenced link. A is the safest fallback if waste cannot be confirmed.

Waste belongs in the recommended statement, as the owner-side consequence of the feedback gap, not as a separate problem.

## 7. Interview questions to validate

**Carinderia owners (8)**
1. How do you know which dishes customers like or dislike today? What do they tell you?
2. Think of a customer who stopped coming. Why do you think they left?
3. Which ulam do you most often have left at closing, and how much is left (portions or pesos)?
4. What do you do with leftovers (reheat, sell next day, give away, throw)?
5. Which dishes sell out first, and how often do you turn customers away?
6. What do you check before raising or lowering a price? How do customers react?
7. Are you on GrabFood, foodpanda, Facebook or Google Maps? Why or why not?
8. How would you feel about customers rating each dish publicly, and what would worry you most?

**Students and workers (6)**
1. How do you choose where and what to eat near school or work, and how much do you spend per meal?
2. Have you ever eaten somewhere and regretted it? What went wrong (taste, price, hygiene)?
3. How do you check whether a stall is clean or safe before eating?
4. Would you rate a dish after eating? What would make it easy enough to do?
5. Would you trust ratings from strangers? What would make you doubt them?
6. Have you ever eaten a dish that was clearly old, reheated or sold out early? What did you do next?
